Instructional Design in Action

Sample 5E Model Lesson Plan

Demonstrating how a classroom topic (e.g. Rate of Chemical Reactions) is structured for maximum student inquiry and engagement.

1. ENGAGE

The Real-World Hook

Start with a provocative visual question or short demonstration: Why do food items spoil faster at room temperature than in a refrigerator?

Focus: Curiosity & Prior Knowledge
2. EXPLORE

Interactive Investigation

Students use PhET Interactive Simulations or lab glow-sticks in hot vs. cold water to observe molecular collision speeds firsthand.

Focus: Student Experimentation
3. EXPLAIN

Collision Theory

Students present their observations. Teacher guides formulation of Collision Theory, Activation Energy, and kinetic variables.

Focus: Student-Led Definitions
4. ELABORATE

Real-World Application

Connecting concepts to industrial catalysts in catalytic converters, enzyme function in human metabolism, and safety in fireworks.

Focus: Cross-Curricular Transfer
5. EVALUATE

Formative Assessment

Exit Ticket (digital Quizizz or 3-2-1 reflection card) to diagnose student understanding and tailor the next instructional step.

Focus: Immediate Feedback Loop

Educational Vision

Inspiring Critical Thinkers, Not Just Exam Takers

Science is not merely memorizing formulas; it is a systematic habit of mind. My goal as an educator is to create a dynamic classroom climate where asking questions is celebrated, errors are treated as essential learning data, and scientific concepts come to life through active experimentation.

1. Inquiry & Scientific Method

Guiding students to hypothesize, test, observe, and defend conclusions based on empirical evidence rather than passive listening.

2. Differentiated & Inclusive Classroom

Recognizing that learners process information differently. Utilizing multi-modal representations (visual, kinesthetic, mathematical) to ensure equitable access to rigorous science.

3. EdTech As a Catalyst

Leveraging interactive digital simulations, LMS platforms, and real-time response systems to make the microscopic world tangible, measurable, and fun.
